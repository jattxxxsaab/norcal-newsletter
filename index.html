📰 Top Stories

What You Need to Know

Interest Rates

Rates at 6.54% — Fed Holds, No Cut Signal in Sight

The Federal Reserve held the federal funds rate steady at its May meeting, and Chair Powell's press conference gave no indication that rate cuts are imminent. The Fed cited persistent services inflation and a labor market that remains too strong to justify easing. Mortgage rates responded: Bankrate's 30-year benchmark rose to 6.54% as of May 3, with Rocket quoting 6.625% APR 6.906%.

The silver lining: forecasters including Morgan Stanley and CAR still project mortgage rates settling near 5.75%–6.0% by Q4 2026, assuming inflation continues its slow grind lower. The path just got bumpier. For buyers on the fence, the May rate environment means roughly $1,850/month principal and interest on a $550K loan — a real number that demands an honest buyer consultation.

🎯 Takeaway: Stop waiting for the Fed to bail your buyers out. Rates in the mid-6s are the market for the foreseeable future. Focus the conversation on long-term value and refi optionality — not the rate.
Market Data

Spring Listings Up — But Not Enough to Cool the Market

Active listings across Northern California are up nearly 10% from Q1 2026 levels as sellers finally shake off the lock-in effect of sub-4% mortgages. Despite that improvement, supply remains historically thin. San Francisco has fewer than 2 months of inventory. Even more affordable Central Valley markets like Stockton and Fresno sit below 3 months — technically still a seller's market.

The slight inventory uptick is good news for buyers who've been losing bidding wars. Multiple-offer situations are becoming less automatic in secondary markets. But in high-demand pockets — well-priced SFRs in Tracy, Mountain House, and desirable Sacramento suburbs — competition remains fierce. Buyers with financing in order are still winning.

🎯 Takeaway: Coach sellers who are hesitating: spring 2026 is still one of the strongest listing environments in years. More listings are coming — get your sellers in front of this week's buyers, not next month's competition.
Investment Spotlight

Bakersfield: The Central Valley's Stealth Appreciation Leader

While Modesto grabbed last week's headline, Bakersfield is running a parallel story worth underwriting right now. With a median home price of $380,000 — the lowest major entry point in California — Bakersfield is posting 8–10% annual appreciation, driven by low land costs, strong population growth, and a rental market where median rents sit around $1,500/month.

The rent-to-price ratio in Bakersfield is among the best in California: a $380K single-family rental at $1,500/month gives you a gross rent multiplier well below statewide averages. New construction remains active but affordable, meaning value-add plays and turnkey rentals are both viable strategies. Kern County's growing logistics and energy sectors are keeping employment steady.

🎯 Takeaway: Bakersfield is the overlooked sibling of the Central Valley story. Lower entry price than Modesto, higher appreciation rate, and a functional rental market. Add it to your investor pitch deck this week.
Regulation Watch

New 2026 Law: AI Listing Photos Now Require Disclosure

Effective January 1, 2026, California agents and sellers are required to disclose when listing photos have been materially altered using AI or digital editing. The law targets changes that affect a buyer's material understanding of the property — removing nearby structures, altering views, adding landscaping or features that don't exist, or using AI-generated staging to replace empty rooms with furniture and decor.

Standard photo corrections — adjusting lighting, color balance, or lens distortion — do not trigger the disclosure requirement. But AI virtual staging and AI-generated exterior alterations clearly do. DRE is actively updating its advisory guidance, and the NAR has flagged this as a compliance priority for Q2 2026. Review your listing photography workflow now.

🎯 Takeaway: If you're using AI virtual staging — and most agents are — add a clear disclosure line to your listing description and marketing materials. Something simple like "Photos include virtual staging" is sufficient. Don't let this catch you at audit time.
🚨 Compliance Alert

AB 628 & AB 414: New Landlord Rules You Need in Your Lease Today

Two bills quietly taking full effect this spring deserve attention from every NorCal landlord. AB 628 requires that all rental units maintain a working stove and refrigerator — a standard that sounds obvious but creates liability exposure if a unit is rented with non-functional appliances. Landlords who fail to repair or replace can face habitability claims.

AB 414 modernizes the security deposit return process: landlords can now return deposits electronically (Zelle, ACH, check scan) and must provide itemized deductions digitally if the tenant requests it. The 21-day return window remains unchanged. SB 610 also adds an obligation to halt rent collection and remove debris when natural disasters affect a rental property — relevant after last year's fire season activity across NorCal.

🎯 Takeaway: Update your lease agreements, tenant move-in checklists, and appliance inspection logs to reflect AB 628. Update your security deposit procedures to comply with AB 414. These aren't gray areas — non-compliance is a straight line to habitability disputes and small claims.
⭐ Central Valley Spotlight

Market to Watch: Bakersfield

Bakersfield — California's Lowest Entry Price, Highest Appreciation Rate

$380K Median  •  8–10% YoY Appreciation  •  $1,500/mo Median Rent

Bakersfield offers the best gross rent yield math in the Central Valley. At $380K purchase price with $1,500/month rent, investors are looking at a gross yield around 4.7% — before appreciation. Factor in the 8–10% YoY price growth and you have a total return story that outperforms most Northern California alternatives on a risk-adjusted basis. The logistics, energy, and agriculture employment base keeps vacancy low. New supply remains active but priced to match demand. This market rewards investors who do the underwriting before it shows up on everyone else's radar.

💡 Agent Pro Tip of the Week

With rates stuck in the mid-6s, your best tool right now is the buydown conversation. A 2-1 buydown (seller-paid) drops a buyer's rate to 4.54% in Year 1 and 5.54% in Year 2 — making monthly payments look dramatically better at the time they matter most. In a market where sellers are increasingly motivated, asking for a seller-paid buydown instead of a price reduction gives the seller a cleaner headline price while getting your buyer real monthly payment relief. Run the numbers in your next listing presentation and buyer consult — this is the move in a 6.54% rate environment.

⚡ Quick Hits

Around NorCal

🏛
SB 79 transit-density rules: rezoning activity accelerating near ACE stations
Effective mid-2026, cities must allow higher-density residential within a half mile of major transit corridors. Tracy and Stockton are seeing early developer interest near ACE train stations. If you have investor clients with commercial land in these corridors, get a zoning consultation now — values will move before the law does.
🤖
Price-fixing algorithms banned in California real estate
New 2026 regulations prohibit the use of shared or coordinated pricing algorithms — the kind used by some property management software to suggest rental increases across portfolios. Property managers using third-party revenue management tools should audit whether those tools share data across competitors. DOJ and state AG are both active in this space.
🏢
Mountain House: $1.05M median holds — Spring sales volume picking up
Mountain House continues to command a premium over surrounding San Joaquin County markets. Post-incorporation momentum is holding. Spring sales volume is trending above last year's pace. Bay Area remote workers remain a primary demand driver — the ACE train commute story is still alive for partial office schedules.
🏭
San Joaquin industrial vacancy stays below 4% — rents up 12% YoY
E-commerce and logistics demand shows no sign of softening in the Central Valley. Distribution center operators continue to absorb new supply as fast as it delivers. For investors holding or pursuing commercial land in the I-5 / SR-99 corridors, this is an exceptionally strong market to be in heading into 2027.
📈
C.A.R. holds 2026 forecast: 274,400 sales, $905K median
California Association of Realtors reaffirmed its 2026 forecast: 274,400 existing single-family home sales (up 2% from 2025) and a statewide median of $905,000 (up 3.6%). Inventory is expected to rise nearly 10% statewide but will not be enough to tip the market to buyers. Affordability sits at 18% statewide — meaning only 18% of households can afford the median home.
